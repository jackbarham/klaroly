Klaroly enquiries, proof of concept

The list from enquiries-research.md: one row per enquiry rather than per event, ordered by neglect rather than by date, with the sparse booking screen behind it rather than a second detail layout. Tap a row to open it. Type in the filter. Arrow keys move through the list on desktop.

Standalone page, not in the codebase. 6 September 2026, and the fixtures are written against that date. Updated after the first review: the stage pill and the stage colours are settled and their controls are kept only so the comparison can be made again, the clash line has moved into the artist's own view menu, and empty sections have a third setting.

Phone

Real pixel sizes with the status bar, page header, sticky filter and floating tab bar all taking their actual space. Tapping a row pushes the booking screen into the same frame, the way /bookings/:id does, because an enquiry and a booking are one record at different stages. The back button returns.

Desktop

Same markup, same components. The split comes from a container query at 760px, which is why it works inside a box on this page. Nothing is selected when you arrive. Scroll the list and watch the detail stay put: it is position: sticky inside the one scroll container, so there is no height calculation anywhere.

What to look at

1. The default order, which is the call the screen turns on

Open the view menu beside the filter box and move Sort through its three settings. Staleness is the default and it is the one that answers the screen's own question: the top of the list is the four people nobody has spoken to for weeks, and Kit Ardern is the first row in the whole app that tells you something you did not know, because that enquiry has been silent for six weeks and the wedding is in six weeks.

Notice the rule that had to be added to make staleness work. A brand new enquiry has the freshest timestamp in the list and would sort to the bottom, which is exactly backwards, so Not looked at yet is pinned above everything and ordered newest first. Two rules instead of one, and the two-rule version is what the artist actually means.

Stage reads as a pipeline and makes the one-tap stage change obvious. It also does the thing the note warned about: Saoirse Gallagher, quoted and silent for over three weeks, sits in the middle of the Quoted group looking like everybody else. Wedding date is the third, and it is the one I would drop, because the calendar already answers that question properly.

2. One row per enquiry, not per event

Two fixtures make the case. Hannah Crewe and Tomasz Nowicki have no wedding date at all, which is a normal and often promising enquiry, and an events-shaped payload cannot represent either of them: there would be no row. Delphine Marchetti has a trial in March and the wedding in May, which is two events and one conversation, and two rows would mean two staleness figures reading the same number and two chances to reply twice to the same person.

So this list needs GET /api/enquiries returning bookings, not a filter over GET /api/events. Section 3 of the note has the reasoning.

3. The stage change on the row, three shapes

Move the Stage change on the row control. The pill is the control makes the thing your eye already goes to tappable, and it costs nothing on the row: tap a stage pill and the sheet offers the four stages plus Convert and Mark as lost. It is two taps rather than one, which is the honest objection to it.

A named chip is genuinely one tap and says where it is going by name. Watch what it costs, because it was measured rather than guessed: with the pill as the control, two of the fourteen second lines truncate on the 375px frame and one on the 393px frame. With the chip, seven of fourteen truncate. Detail only truncates exactly the same two as the pill, which is the number that settles it: making the pill tappable costs nothing at all on the row, and the chip costs five more truncated venues out of fourteen.

One thing the chip taught us that outlives the chip. Its first version offered the next value in the enum, so a New enquiry was offered In conversation, which is the move nobody is in a hurry to make. It now offers Possible, because 5.1 says the soft hold is the interaction the whole feature turns on, and routing New through In conversation makes the one tap into two. Whichever shape wins, that is the rule: the row offers the move that matters, not the next row of the table.

Settled on the pill after the first review, and the control below is kept only so the comparison can be made again. The chip is not being built.

Detail only is the clean list, and it is what 5.1 explicitly rules out: "It has to be one tap from the enquiry row, with no form in the way, or she will not do it." It is here so the cost of obeying that rule is visible beside the cost of ignoring it.

Swipe is not built. It is the fastest of the four and the least discoverable, it needs a second gesture for Lost, and it is awkward on a laptop. Worth revisiting with evidence about how often stages actually move, which is a question for real use.

4. The clash line, which is the new idea

Settled: it ships, on by default, and it is the artist's setting rather than yours. It has moved out of the controls at the top of this page and into the view menu as Show date clashes, beside the source line and the totals. Turn it off and on there. Three enquiries and one confirmed booking all want 12 June 2027, which is the Saturday 5.2 exists for. The line does not prevent anything and it is not a warning; it says three of these four are going to book somebody else in the next fortnight, and the one you ring today is the one that converts.

Open Marta Kowalczyk and look at the panel on the detail. Every competing record carries how long ago it was last touched, which is 5.2's actual point: an enquiry that went quiet three months ago is one you override without a thought, and one that was a conversation last week is a phone call worth making first.

One thing to decide. Marta is at In conversation, so her enquiry holds no date and carries no calendar mark (decision 173 and dayMarks.ts). The clash line still appears on her row, because it describes what is already on the date rather than what this record contributes to it. I think that is right, and it is a deliberate departure from the calendar's rule rather than an oversight, so it is worth your agreement before it goes in a prompt.

One reservation, said once and then left alone. This is the same shape as the amounts-owed switch on contacts, and it has the same problem: a control that hides a double-booked Saturday is a control that gets left off, and the artist then has a calm list and two weddings on the same morning. It is on by default, which is most of the answer, and it is your product. Worth having said.

And the wording had to be cut down. The sentence the note proposed, "Already booked, and two others want this date", truncates on every frame including the 380px desktop column. What fits is "Booked, and two others want it". The long version survives on the detail panel, where 5.2's actual wording has room, so the two say the same thing at two lengths rather than one of them saying it badly.

4a. Two endings, one stage

Turn Show ones not going ahead on in the view menu and look at the group at the bottom. Ottilie Vance says Turned down and the two below say Lost. There is still only one stage behind all three.

It is a reason with a side, not a stage of its own. bookings.lost_reason already exists as varchar(40) with no constraint, so it was going to fill with free text the moment anybody used it. Giving it an enum whose values know whether it was them or you buys the whole distinction and changes nothing else: no new stage, no migration to the check constraint, and no edit to strengthByStage, WaitingOnResolver, either list filter, or any future test of whether a record is still live. The two endings behave identically. Both release the date, both archive the record, and the only thing that differs is who decided, which is exactly what a reason is for.

The word is "Turned down". It is what an artist says out loud, which matters given the promises document says this app gets read aloud. "Rejected" and "refused" read as a judgement on the person and are ambiguous about direction, because a client rejects a quote too. "Unaccepted" is not a phrase anybody uses.

The group heading cannot be "Closed", because that stage is taken by done and paid. "Not going ahead" covers both endings, and the pill on each row says which one it was.

Tap a stage pill and then This one is not going ahead to see the reason list. The heading carries the side, so no reason has to say who did it, and the two "Another reason" rows are not a duplication: which heading they sit under is the fact being recorded. Endings are deliberately two taps. 5.1 asks for one tap to Possible and nothing asks for one tap to an ending.

What this is actually worth is not the label. It is that the conversion rate stops lying, because a job you turned down comes out of the denominator and the figure then measures how well you sell rather than how often you were free. And turned away becomes a number of its own, which is the most useful pricing signal in the product: an artist turning away six Saturdays a year has a rate card that is too low, and nothing else in the app can tell them so.

Ottilie Vance is on 12 June 2027 on purpose. That is the contested Saturday, and turning an enquiry down is the moment the other names on that date matter most. The clash panel already knows about them.

5. Gone quiet after how many days

Move the Gone quiet after control and watch the top group grow and shrink. This is not a display setting: it drives the artist_enquiry_cold branch that already exists in App\Services\WaitingOnResolver, so whatever number goes here is the number the home screen's attention block will use as well.

Settled that it becomes an account setting rather than a constant, and the number itself is deferred. It is not a per-device view preference like the sort, because two screens read it and it describes the business rather than the browser, so it belongs on the account with the rest of the settings groups. The number is a thing to live with for a while and then choose. Seven days is worth seeing before deciding, because six of fourteen enquiries land in the alarming group and the group stops meaning anything.

And there is a disagreement to settle. The resolver reaches artist_enquiry_cold only for an enquiry at Possible. This list colours the age on any live enquiry that has gone quiet, including Saoirse Gallagher at Quoted and Kit Ardern at In conversation, both of which are more urgent than most of the Possible ones. Either the resolver widens or the list is showing something the rest of the app disagrees about, and the second of those is how two screens start telling the artist different things.

6. The detail is the booking screen, and it is nearly empty

This follows from decision 4.3 rather than being a choice: one table, a stage column, everything else nullable, so there is no separate enquiry screen to keep in step with the booking one. What it exposes is that the booking screen has to be good when it is nearly empty, and 19.3 says the summary must be complete on its own and readable at 5am without scrolling.

Move Empty sections on the detail through its three settings with Rosie Duthie open. She arrived this morning through the website and has a name, a date and a venue.

Draw them all is nine sections saying nothing, which pushes the 5am summary below the fold on the most-used screen in the product. Omit them is honest and slightly too quiet: nothing tells a new artist that agreements or intake forms exist at all.

When they are useful is the third option and it is the recommendation. A section appears when the stage makes it the next useful thing, and when it appears empty it carries the action rather than a full stop. An enquiry gets dates, party, price, notes and activity. The moment it converts, Agreement and Payments appear saying no agreement yet, send one, which is discovery and a working control at the same time, arriving at the exact moment it is the right thing to do.

And the teaching that the nine headings were being asked to do costs one sentence instead, at the foot of the screen: "Price appears once this moves to Possible. Payments, the intake form, agreement, messages and photos appear once this becomes a booking."

The argument that settles it is one of yours. Section 21 and section 6 say the app only ever asks about things the artist has switched on: with invoicing off, nothing is ever waiting on a deposit. So a section for a feature that is switched off must not be drawn at any stage, which means "draw them all" can only ever mean "draw the ones already enabled" — and those are exactly the features the artist already knows about. The discovery argument and the toggle rule cancel each other out. This prototype has no feature toggles in it, so that half of the rule is stated here rather than shown.

The one block worth keeping whatever you decide is What they said. It is 5.5.1's rule that the source is kept on the record, and it is the difference between a name from four months ago and a conversation you can pick up.

7. What the artist owns, and what you do

Same split as contacts. The sort, the source line, the totals, the clash line and whether the ones not going ahead show are view settings on the device in localStorage, so there is no column, no migration and no request; change one and reload this page and it comes back as you left it. The controls at the top of this page are yours rather than the artist's.

Three tiers, and it is worth naming them because they keep getting confused. A view setting lives in localStorage and describes this browser: the sort, the four switches. An account setting lives on the account and describes the business: the gone-quiet threshold, because two screens read it. A decision lives in the code and describes the product: whether the stage pill is tappable at all. Five settings in that menu is close to the limit, which is why the clash line went in and a detail-only third state for it did not.

The view menu is built on the measured-anchor precedent from decision 231 rather than on ui/Sheet.vue, and so is the stage menu. That is now two panels hanging off a measured button in this prototype and two in the app, which is the point at which 231 says to extract AnchoredSheet with both callers moving at once.

8. Three numbers this prototype exists to report

  • The tappable pill is free and the chip is not. Two truncated second lines out of fourteen at 375px with the pill or with nothing, seven with the chip. That is section 3, and it is the clearest measurement on the page.
  • 380px is still not enough for the list column. Contacts reported 360 as slightly tight and suggested 380 or 400. This screen is at 380 and truncates the same two rows the 375px phone does, because The Glasshouse at Ravensmere is twenty-eight characters and the date takes the first ninety pixels. Either the column goes to about 420, or a long venue name is accepted as something that truncates, which is a perfectly reasonable answer. It is reported rather than decided, per finding 198.
  • At a seven day threshold the middle band disappears. Gone quiet and Over a week overlap, so the list goes straight from Gone quiet to This week. That is correct behaviour rather than a bug, and it is worth seeing before the number is chosen, because seven days produces a list where six of fourteen enquiries are in the alarming group and the group stops meaning anything.

9. Two things the build found that the note did not

  • The row cannot be a <button>. The contacts row is one, and it works there because nothing inside it is interactive. 5.1 puts a control inside this row, and a button inside a button is invalid markup that browsers resolve by quietly dropping one of them. So the row is a div with role="button", its own key handling, and controls that stop the click reaching it. That is a real cost of obeying 5.1 and it belongs in the prompt rather than being discovered during it.
  • The info family was missing from the prototype's tokens. contacts-poc.html lifted layer two from tokens.css and did not need info, so it did not carry it, and this page inherited the gap. The four values are back, lifted from app.css rather than invented, which is what the style guide requires: if something has no token, stop and ask rather than making one up. Worth knowing that the prototype's copy of the theme is a subset and not the theme.

10. The filter

Type ferndale and it matches through the second line. Type quoted and it matches the stage. Type voice and it finds the enquiries that arrived as a voice note. None of this touches the server, which is section 3 of the note: perhaps twenty to forty live enquiries at any moment, one payload, everything else on the device, and it all works in a hotel basement with no signal.

Not built, on purpose: bulk actions with a checkbox column, saved filters, replying from the list, the follow-up reminder from 5.6.4, source analytics, and anything to do with AI intake. Section 10 of the note says why each one waits. The first three are what a list like this grows by default and thirty rows do not need any of them.

What is fake in here

The data, and only the data. Every person and every venue is invented, which is the rule from Prompt 12 that contacts-poc.html broke: fixture strings reach screenshots. The tokens are lifted from tokens.css, the list row is the style guide's list row, the group headings are the Prompt 12 sticky pattern, the split is the --container-split token, and the stage names, the sources and the waiting-on values are the enums in api/app/Enums rather than anything invented here.
