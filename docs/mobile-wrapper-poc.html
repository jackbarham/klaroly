Klaroly mobile wrapper UI, proof of concept

The chrome only: what sits at the top of every phone screen and what sits at the bottom of every phone screen. The tab bar loses its centre plus and gains Contacts, Home becomes Summary, and a floating bar carries the business name or the screen name on the left with notifications and new on the right. The content underneath is thin on purpose and is not a proposal; it is there so the bars have something to float over and something to scroll.

Standalone page, not in the codebase. 6 September 2026, revised the same evening. Decisions 2026-09-06.2046 to .2110, and prompts/21-mobile-wrapper.md is written against it. The icons are the path data from app/src/components/ui/Icon.vue, not drawn here, so what you are looking at is the set the app already has. Fixtures carried over from home-poc.html and enquiries-poc.html, and the business name is the one the repository's own tests use. Proportions referenced against the FreeAgent iOS app, which is native and uses system materials: the pill shape, the smaller icons and the active tab fill come from it, the glass does not.

The wrapper

Both frames are live and independent. Tap the tabs, the bell, the plus and More. The left frame is a small phone with a longer business name, which is where the top left runs out of room; the right frame is a current phone with a home indicator. Only Summary shows the business name, because the other four say which list you are in instead.

2. The title in the bar, and what it costs

The bar says the screen name on Bookings, Enquiries, Contacts and More, and the business name only on Summary. The reasoning: the business name is identity and identity is worth one screen rather than five, while what an artist needs at the top of a list is which list they are in. The cost is that PageHeader's h1 would say it a second time, so on a phone that heading gives way to the bar. The Title control keeps the two alternatives so the choice can still be reversed by looking rather than by memory.

3. The plus moved, and it costs something

The centre of the tab bar is the easiest target on a phone and the top right is the hardest. FreeAgent, the reference, keeps a green circle at the bottom right and puts search and the bell at the top, so the screenshots are two create affordances rather than one. The argument for moving it anyway is that create is not frequent for an artist with forty weddings a year, and a bar of five destinations with no interruption in the middle reads better and is easier to explain. The mitigation is already in the app: the create sheet comes up from the bottom, so the reach is the first tap and everything after it is back in the thumb zone.

4. Smaller icons, same target

The style guide's fifth signed-off deviation puts a 44px minimum on controls at phone width. The top right buttons look 38px and are hit at 44px through a negative inset on a pseudo element, so the FreeAgent proportions do not quietly cost the tap target. The tab items are 48px tall inside a 60px bar and were never in danger.

5. Summary rather than Home

The screen's own heading has said "Your summary" since home-poc.html, so Home was the odd word out. Summary is the longest of the five labels at 10px and it fits at 375px, which was the only hard question. One label serves both navigations, so the sidebar says Summary too: two names for one destination is worse than a changed word on the desktop.

6. Two things still open

  • Search has nowhere to go. The plus took its place in the pill. Three icons do fit at 375px, which the Search control shows, but the pill then squeezes a long business name. The alternative is a search field at the top of each list, which is what the enquiries and contacts prototypes already assume.
  • Cards on a tinted background. FreeAgent's chrome reads as floating because the page behind it is tinted and the content is in white cards. The style guide has no card component, on purpose, having taken Lemon Squeezy's divider rows on white. The Background control switches between them. This is the decision that would ripple furthest and it is deliberately not part of the build.

7. What this does not settle

  • Notifications. The bell is a container. Decisions 101 to 107 are the real answer, and the badge here is a placeholder: nothing counts it yet.
  • Whether the tab bar hides on scroll. It does not, and the content clears it with a spacer.
  • Anything about the content of the five tabs. The rows here are one shared treatment, deliberately, and the real screens each disagree with it.
  • The desktop, which keeps its sidebar, its own New button and its own page headings, and never sees this wrapper.

Sticky group headings pin to the bottom of the floating bar, which is right while the bar is pinned and slightly wrong while it is hiding on scroll: the heading holds its 52px offset and a gap opens above it. That is a real conflict between the two behaviours rather than a bug in this file, and it is one more argument for the bar staying put.
